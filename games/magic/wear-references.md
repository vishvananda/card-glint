# Played-card surface references

The game-neutral renderer's worn-gloss treatment was informed by these Magic
card condition references. No reference photographs or card artwork are bundled
in the showcase. The pack's [fan notice and asset sources](NOTICE.md) apply.

- [Quiet Speculation: Playing the Damaged Lottery](https://www.quietspeculation.com/2020/12/playing-the-damaged-lottery/)
  shows played Magic cards with uneven white border loss and corner abrasion.
- [Magic Power Nine listing, photographed card back](https://www.ebay.com/itm/146904152969)
  provided a close view of clustered pale chips, mostly short directional
  scratches, heavier corner/border abrasion and surviving surface gloss.
  Reference only; listing availability may change.
- [TCGplayer: Understanding Card Condition Imperfections](https://help.tcgplayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/26141121045143-Understanding-Card-Condition-Imperfections-A-Comprehensive-Guide)
  distinguishes edgewear (loss of color, gloss or material), scratches,
  cloudy scuffing, play dirt and surface wear exposing underlying cardstock.

The shader approximates a much-played but intact glossy card: broken edges,
rubbed corners, sparse exposed fibres and varnish scratches. It intentionally
does not simulate folds, tears or water damage. Gloss remains neutral rather
than adding rainbow foil to ordinary printed ink. The pattern is procedural,
not a reproduction or scan of any referenced card.
