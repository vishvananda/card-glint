# card-glint 0.4.20

The Dark's original black and red mana glyphs now use a measured ink expansion
in both costs and rules text. Source preparation identifies the disc color
from opaque pixels, avoiding a fixed rim probe that can accidentally sample
the skull. Prepared glyphs stay cached; animation adds no decoding or readback.
Public interfaces are unchanged.

The fit uses original printing inputs and frozen frame alignments. Four
diagnostics balance filled overlap, continuous ink coverage, ink area and
outline agreement. A stronger red proposal was rejected because it added too
much ink. Scan blur is modeled only during comparison; rendered symbols remain
sharp. Shared values are selected on ordinary calibration cards and then
checked on separate ordinary and gold groups. These are repeatedly inspected
development groups, not a new holdout.

On the ordinary validation group, filled-glyph overlap improves from
89.10 to 91.90 / 100 for black and 87.26 to 89.81 / 100 for red. Gold-frame
glyphs improve as well. Red's outline overlap remains slightly worse; the
numeric evidence retains individual regressions and the existing whole-card
metric. These indices measure similarity, not percentages of correctness.
The whole-card structural index is essentially unchanged, 77.095 to 77.098 /
100 across 121 qualified scans: 24 improve, 19 regress and 78 are unchanged.

All 122 production DRK faces match the native candidates. Another 194 corpus
faces and 84 unrelated printing controls remain identical. Changes are confined
to mana costs and rules symbols. Disc geometry and existing rendered paper
medians are preserved. The proposed white-disc correction was rejected after
a known-color blur control showed bias in the estimator.

Type/body calibration for land and gold cards, generic numeral shapes and
inline-disc sizing/placement remain open. Existing body-layout differences
remain visible on Drowned and Electric Eel. Ball Lightning remains unscored;
shifted-ink Gaea's Touch 77† remains outside geometry fitting. The frame-score
strip also overlaps two changed inline symbols; those score changes do not
indicate a frame improvement. Hosted scores have not been replaced.
